02 / PROCESS

What the simulator represents

01

Exhaust source

A modeled exhaust-gas stream carries a user-defined particulate concentration.

02

Particle capture

A capture-efficiency parameter estimates the fraction of particulate matter collected.

03

Separation

The captured stream is treated as a carbon-rich solid fraction for the educational model.

04

Purification

Recovery efficiency represents losses during cleaning and conditioning of the carbon-rich material.

05

Ink formulation

The recovered carbon becomes the modeled pigment mass; binder/vehicle is added to reach the selected solids fraction.

03 / ENGINEERING MODEL

Mass balance & assumptions

How the calculation works

Gas flow is converted from L/min to m³/min. The particulate loading is multiplied by gas flow and simulation time to estimate particulate mass entering the capture stage.

Capture efficiency gives the collected mass. Purification recovery then estimates the usable carbon fraction. Finally, the chosen ink solids fraction determines the total formulated ink mass.

PMin = Q × CPM × t
Cusable = PMin × ηcapture × ηpurify
Ink = Cusable ÷ solids fraction

Model limitations

  • Particulate matter is treated as a carbon-rich fraction; real exhaust contains multiple species.
  • Real capture systems require particle-size, temperature, pressure-drop and filter/adsorbent data.
  • Purification chemistry and ink rheology are not experimentally modeled here.
  • Inputs are illustrative and should be replaced with measured laboratory/field data.
For your viva: present this as a digital process model / feasibility simulator, then compare it with future experimental measurements.
